Shuttersmith

Shuttersmith is the first client on our productised SEO + GEO service — built to get a local business found by both classic search and the AI assistants now answering “who does this near me” before a search result ever loads.

Client  Shuttersmith Service  SEO + GEO Category  Local business · Services Status  Active engagement

The problem

Shuttersmith sells and installs plantation shutters — the kind of business where nearly every job begins with someone searching for a supplier near them. It had been running on word of mouth and a site that had never had a real technical SEO pass: no structured data, no service-area pages, nothing that told a search engine what the business did or where it did it. That is an ordinary enough starting point. What makes it expensive now is that search itself has moved.

A growing share of “who does X near me” questions are answered before a results page ever loads — by an AI Overview, by ChatGPT, by Perplexity. Those answers get assembled from whatever the assistant can read cleanly and trust. A site with no schema, no llms.txt, and no consistent statement of its own services and service area doesn’t get recommended; it gets skipped in favour of a competitor whose site happens to be legible. Ranking on Google is still necessary. It is no longer sufficient.

What we did

Shuttersmith is the first client on our productised SEO + GEO service, and the proving ground for it — Audit, Setup, then Growth, in that order.

The audit came first: a full crawl, indexation checked against Search Console, every piece of structured data validated, and an explicit pass on what AI crawlers could and couldn’t see. The output is a list ranked by impact against effort rather than a generic checklist — which matters on a small business site, where there is a finite budget of changes worth making and the order you make them in decides what the money buys.

The setup work that followed was the unglamorous half. LocalBusiness schema, so the business, its service area and its contact details are machine-readable rather than inferred from page copy. Service-area pages, so the site states where it works instead of leaving it implied. Metadata and Open Graph across every page, a clean sitemap.xml and robots.txt, and analytics — that last one first, so everything after it could be measured rather than asserted. Alongside the on-site work sit the Google Business Profile and a review-generation system: for a local trade, the profile and the reviews attached to it frequently do more work than the website does.

What the numbers show

Since we started tracking in July, the site has had 354 real visits (as of 31 Aug 2026) — and of the ones with an identifiable source, the large majority came from Google search. These are sessions in analytics, not impressions or modelled reach, and they are traffic the business wasn’t capturing before.

They have converted into customer inquiries rather than sitting as page views. The clearest evidence of that is second-order, and not something we could have engineered: the work brought in enough new business that the client backed a second company entirely — a trade-price, self-install spinoff of the same shutters business — and asked us to build that site too.

What we are not claiming yet

The GEO half of the engagement is still in progress — the site’s llms.txt is the next thing to ship — so every number above is Search only. We are not claiming an AI-search win on this site, because we haven’t measured one. Once llms.txt is live and AI referrals show up in the analytics the way they already do across our own products, we’ll report whatever they actually are.

We’ll update this page with full before-and-after numbers as the engagement matures. The honest position today: measurable organic traffic where there had been effectively none, a client who reinvested on the strength of it, and a GEO layer still to land.
