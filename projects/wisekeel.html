WiseKeel

A keel isn’t there to slow the ship down — it’s there so the ship doesn’t roll over the first time it’s under load. WiseKeel runs fixed-price engagements that put AI agents to real, governed use inside a client’s own infrastructure — scoped identity, human approval gates, a full audit trail, and nothing of ours sitting on their monthly bill.

Category  AI · Agent Deployment Engagement  Fixed-price · 4 phases Stack  Static site · Node contact handler Status  Live

The problem

Businesses adopting AI agents keep making the same mistake: full autonomy on day one, permissions inherited by accident from a broad API key, and no way to explain after the fact why an agent did what it did. That’s not leverage — it’s risk with a head start.

What we built

WiseKeel is a five-part deployment framework, each principle named after a piece of a ship: Identity (the keel) gives every agent a verifiable identity and scoped permissions — no shared API keys, no standing access. Orchestration (the helm) coordinates multi-agent workflows across a client’s existing tools instead of replacing them. Knowledge (the chart) grounds every agent in the client’s own documents and systems of record, not the open internet. Governance (the ribs) is a policy, designed jointly with the client, that decides what an agent can do alone versus what needs a human — outside the policy means paused for review, not executed. Audit (the log) is an immutable, timestamped record of every action.

The engagement itself runs four fixed phases — Diagnose, Architect, Deploy, Govern — and there’s deliberately no platform fee: every deployment runs inside the client’s own infrastructure, so there’s no WiseKeel-owned system sitting on their bill every month.

The AI angle

WiseKeel isn’t a product a client installs and self-serves — it’s a design and deployment engagement we run for them: scoping what an agent should be allowed to touch, wiring the audit trail, and structuring the staged rollout so autonomy expands on evidence rather than optimism, inside their own stack rather than ours.

How it’s sold

Four fixed engagement tracks, priced in AUD. A Diagnostic (AU$4,500, 1–2 weeks) maps where an agent would create real leverage, no obligation to continue. Deployment (from AU$18,000, 4–8 weeks) architects and ships the first one to three agent workflows into live systems. Managed Operations (from AU$3,000/month, cancel anytime) is optional ongoing monitoring and policy tuning for teams without the in-house bandwidth to own it. And a standalone Security & Governance Audit (from AU$7,500, 2–3 weeks) reviews AI tooling a business already deployed — including tools WiseKeel didn’t build — against the same five-layer standard.

Two entry points on the site itself: security/compliance leaders start from governance and audit trails; operations leaders start from the backlog and where the leverage actually is.

Proven on our own automations first

Before this was something we sold, it was how we already ran our own agents — and still is. Our invoice-ingestion agent pulls invoices straight out of Gmail, extracts the numbers, and either posts clean ones straight into Invoice Ninja automatically or queues anything uncertain for a human to approve — the exact scoped-autonomy pattern the framework describes, not a demo of it. Billing API runs the Stripe and Brevo side of every product in the portfolio the same way. Ghost Writer drafts and publishes content on a schedule, earning more autonomy as its track record held up rather than getting full trust on day one. It’s been useful enough internally that naming the principles explicitly, and offering the same model to clients, was the natural next step — a client adopts it faster when they can see it written down, not just infer it from how carefully we built their integration.
