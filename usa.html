How the Engagement Works Across Timezones

The async-first model that makes timezone differences irrelevant.

01

Send us two paragraphs on the problem and what success looks like. We reply within 48 hours with a realistic scope, a timeline, and any questions. No pitch call required to get a straight answer.

02

Daily written updates

Every sprint day ends with a written update sent before the US East Coast workday starts. What was built, what was tested, what’s next, and any decisions we need from you. No status calls, no chasing. You can respond async or not at all if everything looks right.

Demo every sprint

At the end of each sprint you get something deployed and clickable — not a slide deck. You can share it with your team, your investors, or your first users. If it’s not right, we discuss it before committing to the next sprint.

Frequently Asked Questions

Common questions from US companies considering working with an Australian AI studio.

How does the timezone difference work in practice?

We work async-first by design. Every sprint day ends with a written update delivered before the US East Coast day starts. You respond on your schedule — we act on it while you sleep. For decisions that need back-and-forth, we use async written threads rather than scheduled calls. In our experience, the timezone gap stops mattering within the first sprint.

What do you charge compared to US agencies?

We don’t publish rates publicly — scope too often drives price for fixed-price engagements. What we can say: Australian market rates billed in USD are meaningfully lower than equivalent US agency rates for the same quality of work. We’ll give you a specific number when you send us a brief.

Do you do NDA and US-style contracts?

Yes. We sign NDAs before detailed conversations if required. Our client agreements are written in plain English and can accommodate standard US contract requirements. We’ve worked with US clients who have their own MSAs and can work within those frameworks.

What if I just want to test with a small first project?

That’s exactly how we structure it. Sprint one is deliberately small — the smallest thing that proves the core idea works. You see something real in 2 weeks, at a low cost relative to the total project. If it’s not right, you’ve spent very little to find out. If it is right, we scope sprint two together.

Who owns the code?

You do. Full source code, infrastructure configuration, and deployed product are handed over at the end of each sprint. We don’t retain any rights to what we build for you.
