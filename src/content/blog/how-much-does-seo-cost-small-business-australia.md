SEO can cost nothing if you do the basics yourself, or it can be a one-off project fee or an ongoing monthly fee if you hire someone. What you pay depends on the condition of your site, how many locations or products you have, how competitive your area is, and whether you want the fixes done or just advice.

## How much does SEO cost in Australia?

There is no single Australian price, because "SEO" covers very different jobs. Providers here generally charge in one of three ways: a one-off project, a monthly retainer, or hourly or day-based work for small fixes.

- **One-off project:** an audit, then a block of fixes to your site (page titles, speed, structured data, sitemap). It suits sites that have never been set up properly.
- **Monthly plan:** ongoing content, monitoring and improvements. It suits businesses in busy markets that want someone watching it regularly.
- **Small jobs:** a handful of specific fixes, priced by the day or by the task.

We're not going to put dollar figures on these, because they vary by provider and change over time. The next section explains why, so you can compare quotes sensibly.

## How much should a small business expect to pay for SEO?

A small local business should expect the cost to reflect its size: a single-location trades business with a simple website needs far less work than an online shop with hundreds of products. Be wary of any quote that doesn't depend on your site at all.

A sensible order of spending looks like this:

- **Free first.** Claim and complete your Google Business Profile, fix obvious problems on your own site, and ask happy customers for reviews.
- **A one-off fix next.** If the technical side is beyond you, pay once to get the foundations right.
- **Monthly last.** Only pay a retainer once the foundations are sound and you know what the monthly work would actually be.

Nobody can promise you a ranking, a number of leads or a spot on page one, and anyone who does is selling something they can't deliver.

## What makes SEO cost more or less?

Four things move the price most: the state of your site, its size, how many places you serve, and how much ongoing work you want.

- **Site condition.** Old or unsupported software can make fixes slower or mean the site can't be repaired in place.
- **Size and type.** Online shops, sites in several languages, and sites with many page templates take more work than a five-page brochure site.
- **Locations.** Each suburb, service area or branch you want to be found in adds pages and listings to set up.
- **Ongoing work.** Writing new content and monitoring results takes continuing time, which is why monthly plans exist.

This is also why a fair quote starts from an audit of your actual site. It's reasonable for the price to change after a deeper look if the site turns out to need more than it seemed. What matters is that the change is explained and you agree to it before any work starts.

## What should I do first this week?

Start with the free fixes, which can be done in a few hours and tell you how much help you need.

- **Search for your business name and your main service plus your suburb.** Note whether your business appears, and what Google shows.
- **Check your Google Business Profile.** Search for your business on Google and look for the option to manage your profile. Make sure your hours, phone number, address, categories and services are correct.
- **Look at your website on your phone.** Does it load quickly? Can you find your phone number and services without pinching and zooming?
- **Check each main page has its own clear title.** Open your page in a browser and read the text in the tab at the top. A tab that says "Home" or shows nothing useful is a sign to fix it.
- **Ask three recent customers for a Google review.** Send them a direct link to your profile so it takes under a minute.
- **Set up Google Search Console if you haven't.** It's Google's free tool that shows whether your pages are indexed (stored in Google's list of pages) and what people search to find you.
- **Get a baseline check.** [Run the free website check](/site-check.html) to see what Google and AI assistants can read on your site, such as HTTPS, sitemap, structured data and speed. It's a quick first look, not a full audit, but it shows whether you have obvious gaps before you talk to anyone.

## When do I need an SEO specialist?

You can do the content, the Google Business Profile and the review requests yourself. Bring in a specialist when the problem is in the site's code or setup: structured data (labels that tell search engines what your business is), sitemaps, robots.txt files, page speed, or old software.

When you ask providers for a quote, ask these questions:

- Will you audit my actual site first, and is the audit free?
- What exactly will be changed, and will you fix it directly or just send me a report?
- Is this a one-off cost or monthly, and what do I get each month?
- What happens to the price if the audit finds something unexpected?

## Frequently asked questions

### Is SEO worth paying for if I'm a small local business?

It can be, but start with the free basics first: a complete Google Business Profile, accurate details and customer reviews. Pay for help when there are technical problems on your site you can't fix yourself. Nobody can guarantee results, so judge any provider on how clearly they explain the work.

### Is monthly SEO better than a one-off fix?

Not necessarily. A one-off project suits a site that has never been set up properly, while a monthly plan suits competitive markets where ongoing content and monitoring matter. Many businesses do the one-off fixes first and decide on monthly work afterwards.

### Why do SEO quotes vary so much?

Because the jobs differ. A quote reflects your site's condition, its size, how many locations you serve and how much ongoing work is included. Two quotes that look different may be covering very different amounts of work, so compare what is included, not just the total.

### Can I do SEO myself for free?

Yes, a lot of it. You can set up your Google Business Profile, write clear page titles, ask for reviews and use Search Console at no cost. The parts that usually need a specialist are technical, like structured data, speed and crawler settings.

### Does SEO also help me show up in AI assistants like ChatGPT?

Often the same groundwork helps: clear content, accurate business details and a site that machines can read. Nobody outside the AI companies knows exactly how these tools choose what to cite, so be wary of anyone promising mentions.
