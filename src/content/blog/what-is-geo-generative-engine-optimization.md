GEO, short for generative engine optimisation, means getting your business cited correctly when someone asks ChatGPT, Claude, Perplexity or Gemini a question you could answer. For most small businesses it isn't a separate project. It's good SEO, plus a few specific checks so AI tools can reach your site and describe you accurately.

## What is GEO, in plain English?

More people now ask an AI assistant a question instead of searching Google and clicking through. When the assistant answers, it may mention businesses and cite pages. GEO is about making sure that if it mentions you, it gets you right, and that it can find you at all.

In our understanding, AI assistants answer from search indexes such as Google, Bing and Brave. So GEO is driven by four things:

- Being indexed in those search engines.
- Letting AI search bots into your site.
- Having content in plain page text that answers questions directly.
- What other sites, reviews and listings say about you.

It is not mainly about backlinks or stuffing in keywords.

## How is GEO different from SEO?

GEO isn't a replacement for SEO. It runs on the same foundation: clean page titles, structured data, a site crawlers can read. It adds a few things classic SEO rarely had to think about:

- **AI-crawler rules in robots.txt.** Many robots.txt files were written before AI bots existed. A broad rule can block GPTBot or ClaudeBot by accident.
- **Answers written to be quoted.** A clear question followed by a self-contained answer is easier for an AI tool to lift accurately than a long page of general prose.
- **Consistent details everywhere.** Your name, services, location and contact details should match across your site, Google Business Profile and directories.

## What should I actually do about GEO?

Start with the basics, most of which cost nothing:

1. **Check you're indexed.** Set up Google Search Console and look at the page-indexing report. Bing has a similar free tool.
2. **Check robots.txt.** Make sure it isn't blocking AI search bots you want to reach you.
3. **Put the answers in plain text.** If your prices, services or opening hours live only in images, PDFs or script-heavy widgets, a crawler can't read them.
4. **Add a short FAQ.** Write the questions customers actually ask, with direct two- or three-sentence answers.
5. **Keep your listings consistent and collect reviews.** Reviews and listings are part of what AI answers draw on.

## Does llms.txt help me get cited?

Not on current evidence. An llms.txt file is a plain-text summary of your site written for AI tools. Google says its Search ignores it, and no major AI search provider has said it reads one. It's cheap to add and some coding tools use it, so we include one, but don't expect it to get you cited. Spend your time on the steps above first.

## What if another business has a similar name to mine?

That can confuse an AI assistant. The practical fix is to say plainly on your homepage and About page who you are, what you do and where, and keep those details the same everywhere. It won't guarantee a correct answer, but it removes the ambiguity you can control.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is GEO replacing SEO?

Nobody can say for sure. What's clear today is that the same groundwork helps in both: a site that's indexed, readable and clear about what you do. Treat GEO as an addition to SEO, not a replacement.

### Can anyone guarantee that ChatGPT will mention my business?

No. Nobody outside the AI companies knows exactly how these tools choose what to cite, so be wary of anyone who promises it. What you can do is remove the technical blockers and make your information clear and consistent.

### Which AI assistants does GEO cover?

The same groundwork applies to ChatGPT, Claude, Perplexity, Gemini and Google's AI answers. Each works a little differently, which is another reason not to rely on any single trick.

### Do I need to pay someone to do GEO?

Not necessarily. Indexing, robots.txt, plain-text answers and consistent listings are mostly things you can check yourself. A specialist is worth it when the problem is technical, or when you'd rather spend the hours on your business.

### Is an llms.txt file worth adding?

It's harmless and quick, but on current evidence it isn't what gets you cited. Do it after the basics, not instead of them.
