An SEO and GEO audit checks whether Google and AI assistants like ChatGPT can find your website, read it and describe your business correctly. It usually turns up a short list of fixable technical problems, not a reason to redesign. Most sites are built to look right in a browser, not to be read by a search or AI crawler, and that gap is what an audit finds.

## What does an SEO and GEO audit check?

On the SEO side (getting found in Google):

- Page titles and meta descriptions (the text that shows in search results).
- Canonical tags, which tell Google which version of a page is the real one.
- Open Graph images, the preview picture shown when your page is shared.
- Your sitemap and robots.txt file, which tell crawlers what to read.
- Page speed and image sizes.
- Structured data, a hidden label in the page code that says what the page is (a business, a product, a list of questions and answers).

On the GEO side (being cited correctly by AI assistants):

- Whether AI search bots such as GPTBot and ClaudeBot are allowed in.
- Whether your key information sits in plain page text that answers questions directly.
- FAQ markup written so an answer can be quoted without being distorted.

## What problems turn up most often?

These are common, and each is a fix, not a rebuild:

- **Missing or wrong structured data.** Some sites have none. Others copy it from a template that still names the wrong business, or label a page as a business but never mark up the FAQ section sitting right below it.
- **A robots.txt file that blocks the wrong bots.** A rule written years ago to keep out scrapers can also shut out AI search bots. Nobody chose that, so the fix is usually a few lines.
- **Thin, missing or duplicated titles and descriptions.** Every page says the same thing, or the text is blank or cut off. It shows up in search results, social previews and browser tabs.
- **Answers buried in images, PDFs or script-heavy pages.** If the answer isn't in plain page text, a crawler can't quote it.
- **Slow pages and oversized images.** Easy to miss on a fast office connection.

## Do I need an llms.txt file?

Not on current evidence. An llms.txt file is a plain-text summary of your site written for AI tools. Google says its Search ignores it, and no major AI search provider has said it reads one. We still add one to a site because it takes minutes and some coding tools read it, but we don't treat it as the thing that gets you cited. The bigger wins are being indexed, letting AI search bots in, and having clear answers in plain text.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is the SEO and GEO audit free?

Yes. The audit is free and gives you a prioritised, plain-English list of what to fix. You decide whether to have us do the fixes.

### Will an audit get me to the top of Google or into ChatGPT's answers?

No audit can promise that. Nobody outside Google and the AI companies knows exactly how rankings or citations work. An audit removes the technical blockers so your site can be found and read correctly, and the rest depends on your content, reviews and competition.

### Can the problems be fixed on Wix, Squarespace or WordPress?

Many can, such as titles, descriptions and some markup. Others depend on how much access your platform gives. Tell us which platform you use and we'll say what can and can't be changed.

### What is robots.txt, and why does it matter?

It's a small file at the root of your site that tells crawlers what they may read. If it blocks the wrong bots, Google or AI assistants may never see your pages. It's one of the first things we check because the fix is usually quick.

### How is this different from the free website check?

The website check is a fast first look at what's publicly visible. A full audit goes deeper, using Search Console data, your Google Business Profile and a wider crawl.
